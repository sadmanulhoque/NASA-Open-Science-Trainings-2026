Lesson 3: Planning for Open Science
1. What is Open Science Planning?
Open science means making research easier for others to access,
understand, check, and reuse.

Planning helps a research team decide: - What data, code, and results
can be shared. - Which tools and repositories to use. - When research
products will be released. - Who is responsible for each task. - How
contributors will receive credit.

Open science should be considered throughout the whole research process.

Research is not always linear. Teams may create and share useful
products at different stages.
3. Example: James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)
The JWST research team used open science practices throughout its work.
Early sharing of research data helped scientists detect carbon dioxide
in the atmosphere of an exoplanet.

The open approach also helped the research community produce more than
40 publications.

Key point: Sharing research early can help other scientists check
results, build on discoveries, and make new findings.
4. Open Science and Data Management Plan (OSDMP)
An OSDMP is a plan explaining how a research project will manage and
share its data, software, publications, and other research products.

It identifies: - What products the project will create. - How and where
they will be stored and shared. - When they will become available. - Who
is responsible for managing them.

A good OSDMP supports transparency, effectiveness, reproducibility,
preservation, and quality.
5. Main Parts of an OSDMP
A. Data Management Plan (DMP)
Explains how research data will be handled. It should describe:
- Data types and formats: What data will be created, its size,
  format, and standards.
- Repositories and sharing dates: Where and when data and metadata
  will be shared and preserved.
- Exceptions: Which data cannot be shared and the legal or policy
  reasons.
B. FAIR Principles
FAIR principles help people and computers find, access, understand, and
reuse research data.
- Findable: Data has a unique identifier, useful metadata, and can
  be found through searches.
- Accessible: People can access the data through clear and
  reliable methods, subject to any restrictions.
- Interoperable: Data uses common formats and standards so it can
  work with other systems.
- Reusable: Data has clear descriptions, documentation, and usage
  information so others can use it again.
C. Software Management Plan (SMP)
Explains how project software will be developed, managed, preserved, and
shared.

It should include: - Types of software that will be created or
updated. - Development tools and good practices, such as documentation,
testing, dependencies, and version control. - Where and when software
will be shared. - The software license. - Any software that cannot be
shared and why.
D. Open Science Plan (OSP)
Explains how publications and other open activities will be shared.

Publication sharing may include: - Peer-reviewed papers and technical
reports. - Conference materials and books. - Preprint servers. - Public
repositories, such as NASA's STI Repository. - Open-access journals.

Other activities may include open workshops, preregistering research
plans, open science training, and citizen science projects.
E. Roles and Responsibilities
The plan must identify who is responsible for carrying out each part of
the OSDMP. Responsibilities may include managing data, maintaining
software, sharing publications, and preserving research products.
6. Not Everything Can Be Shared
Open science encourages sharing, but some research products may need to
remain private or restricted because of laws, policies, agreements, or
other limitations.

Before sharing, ask: - Can this research product be shared? - Who helped
collect or create the data? - Who may benefit from sharing it? - Who has
the authority and responsibility to make sharing decisions? - Should it
be shared, and are there any restrictions?

Always follow the rules of your organization and funding agency.
7. Quick Summary
- Open science is part of the entire research workflow: planning,
  implementation, and release.
- An OSDMP explains how data, software, and publications will be
  managed and shared.
- FAIR means Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable.
- A good plan defines products, repositories, timelines, restrictions,
  and responsibilities.
- Sharing should be planned carefully; not every research product can
  or should be public.
8. Activity 3.1
Think about a research project you have worked on:
1. Did your team create a Data Management Plan or OSDMP?
2. How did you manage the data and results?
3. How did you share them?
4. What were your responsibilities?
