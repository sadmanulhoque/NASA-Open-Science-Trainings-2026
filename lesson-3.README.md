# Lesson 3: Planning for Open Science

## 1. What is Open Science Planning?

Open science means making research easy to access, understand, verify, and reuse.

Planning helps researchers decide:
- What data, code, and results can be shared.
- Which tools and repositories to use.
- When research products will be released.
- Who is responsible for each task.
- How contributors will receive credit.

## 2. Three Phases of Open Science

| Phase | Activities | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Develop ideas and prepare the project | Proposals, data management plans |
| Implementation | Collect, organize, and analyze data | Raw data, metadata, code, notebooks |
| Release | Preserve and share results | Papers, archives, talks, blogs, videos |

Open science is part of the entire research process, not just the final stage.

## 3. Example: James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)

The JWST research team followed open science practices throughout its research.

Early data sharing helped scientists detect carbon dioxide in the atmosphere of an exoplanet.

The project also contributed to more than 40 publications.

**Key point:** Sharing research helps other scientists verify results and make new discoveries.

## 4. Open Science and Data Management Plan (OSDMP)

An OSDMP is a plan explaining how research data, software, publications, and other products will be managed and shared.

It describes:
- What products will be created.
- Where and how they will be stored.
- When they will be shared.
- Who is responsible.

### Benefits
- Transparency
- Effectiveness
- Reproducibility
- Preservation
- Quality

## 5. Main Parts of an OSDMP

### A. Data Management Plan (DMP)

Explains how research data will be managed.

It includes:
- Data types, formats, sizes, and standards.
- Repositories and sharing timelines.
- Data that cannot be shared and the reasons.

### B. FAIR Principles

FAIR principles help people and computers find, access, understand, and reuse research data.

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Findable | Data can be easily found using identifiers and metadata. |
| Accessible | Data can be accessed through clear methods. |
| Interoperable | Data uses common formats and standards. |
| Reusable | Data has proper documentation for future use. |

### C. Software Management Plan (SMP)

Explains how research software will be developed, managed, preserved, and shared.

It includes:
- Expected software types.
- Development tools and methods.
- Documentation, testing, and version control.
- Software repositories and sharing timelines.
- Software licenses.
- Software that cannot be shared and why.

### D. Open Science Plan (OSP)

Explains how publications and other research activities will be shared.

Examples:
- Research papers and technical reports.
- Conference materials and books.
- Preprint servers.
- NASA's STI Repository.
- Open-access journals.

Other activities include:
- Open workshops and meetings.
- Registering research plans in advance.
- Open science training.
- Citizen science projects.

### E. Roles and Responsibilities

The OSDMP must identify who is responsible for:
- Managing research data.
- Maintaining software.
- Sharing publications.
- Preserving research products.

## 6. Not Everything Can Be Shared

Open science encourages sharing, but some research products may have restrictions due to laws, policies, or agreements.

Before sharing, ask:
1. Can the research product be shared?
2. Who helped collect the data?
3. Who will benefit from sharing?
4. Who has authority over the data?
5. Are there any restrictions?

Always follow your organization's and funding agency's rules.

## 7. Quick Summary

- Open science has three phases: Planning, Implementation, and Release.
- OSDMP explains how research products are managed and shared.
- FAIR means Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable.
- DMP manages research data.
- SMP manages research software.
- OSP manages publications and other open activities.
- Roles and responsibilities identify who handles each task.
- Not all research products can or should be shared.
