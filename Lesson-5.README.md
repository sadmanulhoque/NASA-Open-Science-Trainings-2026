# Tools for Open Science

## Overview
Open science tools help you share research in a clear, useful, and lasting way.  
These tools support openness at every step of research.

Common tools include:
- Preprints
- Repositories
- Version control
- Persistent identifiers
- Licenses

## Preprints
A **preprint** is an early version of a research paper shared online before (or sometimes after) peer review.

### Why use preprints?
- Share findings quickly
- Get early feedback
- Free for anyone to read
- Help science move faster

### Common preprint servers
- arXiv (physics, math)
- bioRxiv (biology)
- medRxiv (medicine)

**Important:** Preprints are **not** a replacement for peer review. Read them with care.

### Pros
- Fast sharing
- Free to upload and read
- Early community feedback

### Cons
- May contain errors not yet caught by peer review
- Some people see them as less reliable
- A few journals do not accept papers that were first shared as preprints

## Repositories
A **repository** is a safe place to store and share data, code, papers, and other research outputs.

### Why use repositories?
- Better than email or personal websites
- Make your work easy to find and cite
- Support long-term storage

### Types of repositories
- General (example: Zenodo)
- Domain-specific (for certain fields)
- Institutional
- Software repositories (example: GitHub)

### Tips for choosing a repository
- Check if your funder or institution requires a specific one
- Prefer repositories that are widely used in your field
- Make sure the repository supports open access and long-term preservation

## Version Control
**Version control** tracks changes to files (especially code) over time.

### Why it matters
- See what changed and when
- Go back to earlier versions if needed
- Make collaboration easier
- Keep a clear history of the work

### Common tools
- Git (the system)
- GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket (platforms that use Git)

## Persistent Identifiers
A **persistent identifier (PID)** is a long-lasting link to a digital object or person.

### Common types
- **DOI** – for papers, data, and software
- **ORCID iD** – for researchers (links you to your work)

These help people find and correctly cite your research for a long time.

## Licensing
A **license** tells others what they can and cannot do with your work.

### Why licenses matter
- Protect your rights
- Make it clear how others can reuse your work
- Support open science by allowing sharing and building

Choose open licenses with few restrictions when possible (for example, Creative Commons or open-source software licenses).

## Key Takeaway
Use these tools to make your research easier to find, share, reuse, and trust.  
They help science become more open and useful for everyone.
